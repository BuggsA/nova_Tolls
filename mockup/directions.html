Worth the Toll? Pick a direction

Six looks, each showing the same two things: the answer for a saved morning trip, and the November scoreboard. The numbers are samples. Each one is built to be read at a glance, with no decoration you have to look past. Pick one, or mix parts ("the answer from 4, the scoreboard from 2").

What makes a site look AI-built

  • Every block is a rounded white card with a soft shadow
  • Tiny gray uppercase labels above everything
  • Pills, chips and emoji (🔥) scattered around
  • Too many sections that all have equal weight
  • Gimmicks like the glowing LED sign
  • Default looks: purple gradients; cream with a serif and an orange accent (the look of Claude's own site, now copied everywhere); black with one neon green

What these do instead

  • One clear answer at the top, in the largest type on the screen
  • Numbers you can read from arm's length
  • Borrowed from places people already read quickly: road signs, transit apps, government forms, car dashboards
  • Few colors, each with one job (green means take it)
  • Fewer boxes: lines and spacing do the grouping
Direction 1

Road sign

The answer looks like the green sign you'd see over the highway. The type is Overpass, a cousin of the lettering on US highway signs. The toll sits in a black panel like the real price signs. Flat, high contrast, no shadows.

Why it reads fast
Drivers already know how to read highway signs in a second.
Watch out for
It can feel like a novelty if pushed too far. Keep the sign style to the answer and the scoreboard.

Direction 2

Departure board

Built like the best transit apps: a short list with very big times. Express and regular sit side by side like two buses, and the winner is shaded. Each road gets its own color badge (I-66 orange, 495 blue, ICC purple).

Why it reads fast

Watch out for
Needs strict spacing to stay tidy as more roads and trips are added.

Direction 4

Plain and official

Based on the US government's web design system, which is built to be read fast by everyone, including on old phones and in bright sun. Plain black text, one colored bar for the answer, real tables, real buttons. It feels trustworthy, like the toll authority should have built it.

Why it reads fast
Nothing to decode. Every number is labeled in plain words.
Watch out for
It can look like a government page. It would need a strong logo and a little warmth to feel like its own site.

Direction 5

Dashboard

Dark, like a modern car's screen. The main reading is a gauge: your cost per minute, with your limit marked on the dial. It looks great at night and in the car.

Why it reads fast
One dial and one word. The needle shows where you stand without any math.
Watch out for
Dark-only sites can look generic (black with a neon accent is a common AI look). It needs a light version for daytime and real restraint.
